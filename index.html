BRAND CONTEXT

Research Subject: TALKLOOK & Urban Escape

Founded in 2019, TALKLOOK is a Ningbo-based lifestyle composite brand advocating “freedom without restraint,” communicating a positive way of life through different spatial settings. This project focuses on the community-store transition of its Urban Escape sub-brand.

SUB-BRAND · 01

Urban Escape

A roughly 500 m² new-Chinese-style space in Ningbo's High-tech Zone, serving creative coffee, Chinese tea, and fusion meals, with the promise “Rest freely beyond the city.”

PROJECT · 01

Community-Store Transition

The brand will move from a mall-traffic setting into a residential community. How should a mall-oriented new-Chinese café reposition, acquire customers, and retain them to take root locally — an open question with no standard answer.

CORE CONCEPT

“Community Living Room”
Three ways of life inside one café

Not just grabbing a coffee to go, but inviting residents in as if returning to their own living room — someone can sit quietly alone, a group can talk naturally, and a small team can hold a meeting nearby.

Solo RelaxationNeighborly TalkSmall MeetingsRegular RelationshipsNew-Chinese SpaceCommunity Belonging

Group Talk · Neighbors Together

Long tables and shared seating carry casual chat, interest groups, parent-child and craft activities — where community relationships actually happen.

Small Meetings · Flexible Discussion

Movable tables, whiteboard, and screen form a semi-open discussion zone for freelancers and small teams to meet and do light work nearby.

CONSUMER JOURNEY

Unpacking community-store opportunities along the journey

Following the five research steps from the course brief, we identify behavioral patterns, pain points, and designable touchpoints at each stage from recognition to retention.

1
TOPIC 12 / 4

Recognize · Community Persona

Map nearby residents: families, young office workers, freelancers, retirees, and students — distinguishing their motivations and space preferences.

2
TOPIC 2

Acquire · Neighborhood Reach

Rely on word of mouth, communities, and delivery entry points, plus local outreach and referrals, to convert passersby into first-time visitors.

3
TOPIC 3 / 6 / 13

Perceive · Brand Image

Observe how residents perceive the space, products, and service after the move; build a “neighborhood living room” mindset rather than a mall check-in impression.

4
TOPIC 1 / 7

Product · Scene Fit

Adjust the product mix for solo, social, and meeting scenes: solo sets, sharing platters, meeting break packages, and time-based drinks.

5
TOPIC 8 / 10

Retain · Regular Relationships

Build repeat purchase through membership, community events, and consistent service, turning one visit into long-term neighborly loyalty and frequent return.

Research line: community persona → word of mouth / delivery / communities → post-move brand perception → scene-based product structure → neighborhood repeat purchase and regular relationships.
PROPOSAL

“Community Living Room” Directions to Roll Out

Based on the journey analysis, the studio puts forward initial directions across space, product, acquisition, and relationship; detailed conclusions appear in the final consultancy report.

Space: One Store, Three Zones

  • Quiet Zone · Solo relaxationWindow single seats and bar seats for reading and light work
  • Social Zone · Long tableCombinable tables for neighborhood events and group gatherings
  • Meeting Zone · Small huddlesSemi-open discussion seats with whiteboard / screen / booking

Product: Match by Scene

  • Daily solo setCoffee / tea + light bite, a high-frequency repeat price band
  • Sharing platterDesserts and snacks suited to the long group table
  • Meeting break packTime-slotted booking for small-team huddles

Acquisition: Start with Neighbors

  • Community operationsBuilding / neighborhood groups share events and new items, building private traffic
  • Word of mouth & referralsRegular-customer referral mechanism, paired with delivery platforms nearby
  • Local eventsCraft sessions, talks, parent-child hours tied to the community rhythm

Retention: Turn Customers into Neighbors

  • Recognition & memoryRemember names and favorites, reinforcing the feeling of being known
  • Membership & repeat systemPoints, time-slot benefits, and quarterly event privileges
  • Continuous monitoringTrack visit frequency, event participation, and feedback to iterate space and product
PROJECT RUBRIC

Eight Graded Phases of the Final Project

The CBI Studio final project is assessed across eight phases with the weights below. Our report maps evidence to each anchor, reviewed under the same standards by both AI grading and the Human × AI coach.

01 · OBJECTIVEWeight 13%

Brand, Problem, Opportunity

Clearly articulate the consumer behavior challenge and opportunity.

  • Problem statement defining the core consumer issue (e.g., unclear pricing leading to cart abandonment)
  • Opportunity aligned with market trends (e.g., AI personalization, sustainable consumption)
  • Success metrics set as measurable KPIs (e.g., reduce abandonment by 20%)
  • Root causes drilled into 5 Whys; objective grounded in industry data / reports
02 · CONSUMER RESEARCHWeight 16%

Behavioral Data

Deeply understand consumer behaviors, motivations, and pain points.

  • Qualitative: interviews, focus groups, and/or ethnographic observation
  • Quantitative: surveys, questionnaires, and/or behavioral analytics
  • Secondary data: industry reports or social listening
  • Personas and/or journey maps synthesizing the research
03 · INSIGHT GENERATIONWeight 15%

Synthesized Findings

Translate raw data into actionable insights about consumer behavior.

  • Patterns identified: recurring themes clustered from data
  • Root-cause analysis linking behaviors to underlying causes (e.g., fishbone)
  • Opportunity prioritization: insights ranked by impact and feasibility
  • Insights framed against capabilities (e.g., SWOT, affinity diagrams)
04 · IDEATION & SOLUTION DESIGNWeight 15%

Creative Solutions

Generate creative solutions addressing the consumer behavior insights.

  • Structured ideation (design thinking, brainwriting, SCAMPER)
  • Rapid prototyping: low-fidelity prototypes (wireframes, mockups, scenarios)
  • Consumer co-creation: target users involved in ideation
  • Solutions mapped to consumer jobs-to-be-done (e.g., lean canvas)
05 · TESTING & VALIDATIONWeight 15%

Validate with Consumers

Validate solutions with consumers to ensure they address behavior gaps.

  • Usability testing / A-B testing with measured performance
  • Feedback loops: qualitative feedback gathered and used
  • Pilot testing with a small user subset measuring real-world impact
  • Satisfaction / impact metrics tracked (e.g., NPS before-after)
06 · SCALING & IMPLEMENTATIONWeight 11%

Full-Scale Rollout

Transition validated solutions to full-scale implementation.

  • Phased roadmap (e.g., Month 1: MVP; Month 3: expand)
  • Stakeholder alignment: buy-in from cross-functional teams
  • Post-launch KPI monitoring and iteration plan
  • Project tracking and continuous improvement (OKRs / Kaizen)
07 · ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONSWeight 8%

Protect Consumer Trust

Embed ethics into every phase to protect consumer trust.

  • Data privacy: regulatory compliance (GDPR / CCPA), anonymization, consent
  • Transparency: clear communication of how consumer data is used
  • Inclusivity: solutions serve diverse populations (accessibility)
08 · REPORT STANDARDSWeight 7%

Structure & Academic Standards

Professional consultancy report of ~3,000 words in APA style.

  • Required sections present: Introduction, Problem Statement, Opportunity Identification, Innovative Solutions, Rollout Plan, Conclusion
  • References and appendix included; APA style
  • Appropriate length (~3,000 words) and professional presentation
